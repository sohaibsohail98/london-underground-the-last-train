# Advertising and dressing

The station wants ORIGINAL fictional adverts (no real brands, no real agency
artwork) sitting in correctly sized, correctly placed frames, plus the litter
and clutter that a real Tube station accumulates. This file is about the frames,
the placement rhythm and the clutter, not about designing the posters.

## Standard London Underground poster formats and real dimensions

Poster advertising is sold in "sheet" sizes. The sheet count is historic (a
"sheet" was a Victorian printing sheet). Modern equivalents:

| Format | Metric size (portrait unless noted) | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **4-sheet** | **1016 x 1524 mm** (portrait) | The workhorse. Wall mounted in a frame at concourse and platform level, in gate line areas, on stairs. Usually in runs. |
| **6-sheet** | **1200 x 1800 mm** (portrait) | Backlit floor standing or wall units, more common at street entrances and bus interchange than deep platform. |
| **16-sheet** | **2032 x 3048 mm** (portrait) | Large wall poster, escalator machine chamber walls, big concourse walls. |
| **48-sheet** | **3048 x 1524 mm** (landscape, roughly 6.1 x 3.0 m visible in some installs) | The billboard size. Cross track (see below) and large concourse walls. Frequently quoted as ~3048 x 1524 mm; some cross track sites list ~6.1 m x 3.0 m for the paper across multiple panels. Treat the single unit as 3048 x 1524 mm and gang them for the big ones. |
| **96-sheet** | **12192 x 3048 mm** (landscape) | Double the 48-sheet, only on the largest concourse walls and some cross track runs. |
| **Cross track projector / cross track poster** | Poster is ~**1520 x 1016 mm** (landscape) per panel, ganged in a long run | Mounted on the tunnel wall OPPOSITE the platform, so a waiting passenger reads it across the track. Dwell time on the platform is quoted at around 40 seconds, which is why this site is prized. Sits roughly 1.2 to 2.5 m above the trackbed, in a continuous framed band. |
| **Escalator panels** | Small landscape panels, roughly **500 to 600 mm tall** run continuously up the escalator incline at reading height on the balustrade wall, spaced every ~1.5 to 2 m | The sequential run beside a moving escalator. Now often digital. |
| **Digital escalator panels (DEP)** | Same footprint as the print panel, illuminated screen | Replacing the print run on busy escalators. |
| **Tunnel / platform digital screens** | Landscape LCD, roughly 1.8 x 1.0 m | Platform walls and concourse, near the next train indicator. |
| **Gateline / column wraps** | Vary, wrap a column or clad a gate line end | Concourse. |

Frame construction: aluminium snap frame or a fixed glazed box, mid grey or
stainless, a shallow return of 40 to 80 mm, sometimes backlit (a light box with
a translucent poster face, edge or backlit LED). Cross track frames are a long
continuous extruded aluminium band with glazing and internal fluorescent or LED
strips. All are original, generic frame extrusions, nothing brand specific.

## Placement rhythm

- **Platform wall (behind the passenger)**: 4-sheet frames at fairly regular
  intervals, order of every 4 to 8 m, sometimes paired, broken by columns,
  benches, help points and signage. Not every bay, but a steady beat.
- **Cross track (tunnel wall opposite)**: a near continuous band of cross track
  posters the length of the platform, interrupted by the large station name
  marks and by digital screens. This is the wall the player stares at when the
  train is not there, so it carries a lot of the environmental storytelling.
- **Escalator incline**: continuous panel run both sides, one panel roughly
  every step-and-a-half of travel, so it reads as a flicker book on the way
  down.
- **Concourse and passages**: 16-sheet and 48-sheet on the big flat walls,
  4-sheet in the tighter passages, poster runs on the approach to the gate line.
- **Gate line**: heavy advertising density just before and after the gates,
  where everyone slows and queues.
- **Rule of thumb for the grey box dressing**: platform wall gets a 4-sheet
  frame every ~5 m, the opposite tunnel wall gets a continuous cross track band,
  the escalator gets a panel run, the concourse gets two or three big boards.

## Content direction for the fictional posters

Original brands only. Period and place appropriate: fictional West End shows,
fictional banks and fintech (this is Canary Wharf), fictional streaming
services, recruitment, gambling, energy drinks, a public health campaign, a
missing person or lost pet notice, a byelaw notice, an "improvement works"
apology notice. Age and damage them: torn corners, graffiti tags, a newer poster
slapped over an older one, water damage at the bottom edge, a smashed frame with
the poster sagging, condensation inside a light box, dead tubes so half the
board is dark. The crimson `#B02030` and violet `#6C4C9C` palette can key a
recurring fictional brand so the world feels coherent.

- Simplest path: model the frame as a parametric extrusion in engine (a plane
  for the poster, a chamfered border, an optional emissive backlit plane) and
  drive the poster texture from an array of original art. A 4-sheet frame is a
  five minute mesh.

## Station clutter, what actually accumulates

### Litter types

- Free newspapers (the evening freesheet) folded, dropped, trodden flat, stuffed
  behind seats and into frame gaps, blown against the platform edge.
- Paper coffee cups, cup sleeves, plastic lids, disposable cups.
- Plastic bottles and energy drink cans, rolling in the trackbed.
- Food wrappers, crisp packets, sandwich cartons, a half eaten pastry.
- Receipts, tickets, travelcard wallets, tissues, cigarette ends near entrances
  (smoking is banned inside, so butts cluster at the street door).
- Chewing gum: thousands of flattened black discs on the platform floor, densest
  along the waiting line and at the edge.
- Cable ties, plastic strapping, bits of packaging from restocking the shops.
- Leaves and grit tracked in from the entrances, fanning out from every doorway.
- Dust and fluff ("fluff" is literally what accumulates in the tunnels, a grey
  felt of shed clothing fibres and skin) in the corners and on ledges.

### Abandoned item categories

- Umbrellas, single gloves, scarves, a lone shoe.
- Shopping bags and carrier bags, a suitcase or holdall left by a bench (this is
  also the security concern, an unattended bag, which is good tension dressing).
- A pushchair, folded, against a wall.
- Books and phone chargers on the seats.
- A hi-vis vest, a hard hat, a contractor's tool bag from the works.
- A bunch of flowers, wilting, taped to a column (a memorial).

### Bins

- Since the 1990s security era, deep Tube stations largely removed enclosed
  bins. What you see: a **clear plastic bag on a metal hoop ring** bracketed to
  a wall or a free standing hooped stand, so the contents are visible. Also
  open topped mesh bins on concourses, and modern stainless bins with a
  restricted aperture at the newer stations. Overflowing, bag torn, litter
  spilling around the base.

### Benches

London Underground platform seating design language:

- **Bench form**: a run of individual seats or a continuous bench, wall mounted
  on cantilever brackets or on end legs, tucked into a wall recess or against a
  column. Length from a single seat up to 6 or 8 seats in a run.
- **Materials**: historically timber slats on a cast iron frame in old stations;
  modern London Underground and Elizabeth line uses **moulded or slatted
  timber, or perforated pressed steel, or a hardwood plank seat on a stainless
  or powder coated steel frame**, often with a solid back panel that doubles as
  an advertising or station name panel. Some have arms between seats (to stop
  lying down), some are backless.
- **Perch seats / lean bars**: a sloped padded or timber rail at roughly
  700 to 800 mm, common in high flow areas where a full bench does not fit.
- **Wear**: seat surface polished smooth and slightly hollowed where people sit,
  gum stuck underneath the front lip, litter and free newspapers behind and
  under, scuffed feet marks on the floor in front, the back panel scratched and
  stickered.
- Do NOT reproduce a specific station's heritage bench with its station name
  moulded in; make the back panel an original station mark or leave it blank.

### Other fixed clutter

- Help points (blue and green, button panel plus speaker, on a column or wall).
- Fire equipment cabinets (red, recessed or surface, hose reel and extinguisher).
- Ticket machines and gate line furniture on the concourse.
- Cleaning store cupboards, a mop bucket and a wet floor A-frame sign left out.
- A folded metal barrier or two, "Platform closed" or "No entry" A-boards.
- Cable trays, conduit, a junction box, a CCTV camera on every sightline.
- Pigeon spikes on ledges, and pigeon muck under them, near the entrances.
- Vending machines, a cash machine, a small retail kiosk shutter (closed, for
  the after hours feeling this game wants).
