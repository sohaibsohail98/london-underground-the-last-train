# Signage and wayfinding

Descriptive only. No artwork is downloaded or reproduced. The point is to know
the information structure, the panel geometry and the mounting positions so an
original wayfinding system can be built that sits in the right places and reads
as a real interchange without touching TfL trade dress.

## Off limits, stated plainly

The roundel (bar and circle target), the Johnston and New Johnston typeface, the
official Tube map and geographic line diagram, the Elizabeth line and Jubilee
line official colour identities and stripe arrangements, the official directional
sign artwork. Describe that these exist, never recreate them. Build an original
mark, an original typeface choice, an original colour bar.

## The information hierarchy

Underground wayfinding is layered, coarsest first, and each layer answers one
question:

1. **Line / network identity.** "You are in a station, on this line." Carried by
   the roundel target shape and the line colour. Substitute: an original
   station mark on a coloured bar, palette violet `#6C4C9C` as the house colour.
2. **Direction of travel / destination.** "This platform goes towards X."
   Platform is labelled with the direction (for example "Eastbound", "Westbound"
   or a terminus name) not just a number.
3. **Way out and interchange.** "Exit this way", "Change here for line Y." The
   single most repeated sign in the system is the "Way out" directional. Yellow
   background is the convention for exit routing; keep that idea, it is generic
   safety practice, just do not copy the exact artwork.
4. **Platform number.** Large numeral, high, at the platform entrance and
   repeated along the platform.
5. **Local and safety information.** "Mind the gap", "No exit", "Help point",
   emergency instructions, byelaw notices, gate line signage. Smaller, denser,
   lower.
6. **Commercial and service info.** Next train indicator, line status, maps,
   these sit within the wayfinding frame but are dynamic.

## Panel shapes and proportions

- **Directional signs**: long horizontal rectangles, roughly **3:1 to 6:1**
  width to height, a coloured band top or left carrying the arrow and the
  station mark, then the destination text, then the arrow. Panel heights of the
  order of 300 to 450 mm, lengths a metre or more, ganged into runs.
- **Frieze signs**: a continuous horizontal band running along the wall just
  below the ceiling or above the platform edge screen, repeating the line name
  and station name at intervals. Effectively a long thin ribbon, 250 to 400 mm
  tall.
- **Platform name / roundel position**: a square or circular sign centred on the
  tunnel wall opposite the platform, repeated every 10 to 20 m so a passenger in
  any carriage sees one through the window. At Canary Wharf Jubilee these are
  exceptionally large, around 1 m. Substitute: a large original disc or square
  mark on the wall, same rhythm.
- **Totems and hanging signs**: double sided rectangular blades hung from the
  soffit at circulation decision points, portrait or landscape, roughly
  600 x 900 mm, on stainless drop rods or a bracket.
- **Wall mounted flag signs**: project at 90 degrees from a wall or column at a
  junction so they are read down the corridor.
- **Next train indicator**: a wide landscape enclosure, black face, dot matrix
  or amber LED historically, now white LED, showing two or three rows of
  "destination ... min". Roughly 1200 to 1800 mm wide, 200 to 400 mm tall.

## Mounting positions and heights

- **Hanging / suspended**: soffit mounted blades at decision points and over
  gate lines. Bottom of sign typically **2.1 to 2.6 m** above floor for
  clearance, higher in the big Jubilee volume.
- **Wall mounted directional**: centreline around **1.7 to 2.0 m** above floor,
  at eye level and above, ganged in horizontal runs.
- **Frieze**: high on the wall, **2.4 m and up**, or integrated into the head of
  the platform edge screen doors.
- **Platform wall roundel / station mark**: centred roughly **1.5 to 2.2 m** to
  its own centre, sized so it clears seated passenger eye line through a train
  window.
- **Next train indicator**: suspended over the platform near the entrance and
  repeated mid platform, bottom edge around **2.3 to 2.6 m**.
- **Safety and byelaw notices**: **1.4 to 1.7 m**, wall or column mounted, in a
  simple frame.
- **"Mind the gap" and platform edge text**: on the platform floor and on the
  platform edge screen door glass at head height. Floor text and a repeated
  strip. Describe, do not reproduce the exact lettering.
- **Help point**: a column or wall unit at roughly **1.0 to 1.4 m** for the
  buttons, with a sign panel above, usually blue and green, at each end of the
  platform and on concourses.

## Next train indicator style and placement

- Historically a dot matrix or discrete LED display, amber on black, two lines,
  scrolling. Newer installs are dense white LED or LCD, still black faced.
- Content pattern: line/destination, then a gap, then minutes or "due". A third
  line for a following train or a service message.
- Placement: hung centrally over the platform, first one visible as you step off
  the escalator or through the platform entrance, then repeated once or twice
  down a long platform. At Canary Wharf Jubilee, with platform edge screen
  doors, indicators are also mounted at the screen door head.
- For the game: an original amber on charcoal dot matrix reads as period transit
  without being anyone's brand. Palette sodium `#E0A030` on charcoal `#16161C`.

## Typeface candidates (free, OFL or equivalent, not Johnston)

Johnston's tells are the diamond shaped tittles and full stops and the perfectly
circular O. Avoid those and anything sold as an Underground revival. All below
are free for commercial use and can be committed with their licence text. The
project brief already floats Overpass; `docs/reference/free-assets.md` section E
records Overpass, Public Sans and Barlow as the shortlist. This list adds two
more and gives the character notes.

| Font | Licence | Source | x-height and character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Overpass** (Delve Withrington / Red Hat) | SIL OFL 1.1 | https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Overpass and https://github.com/RedHatOfficial/Overpass | Derived from the US FHWA Highway Gothic series. Tall x-height, open apertures, slightly squared curves, plain round tittles. Reads unmistakably as road and transit signage. Has Overpass Mono for departure boards and platform numbers. Best primary choice. |
| **Public Sans** (USWDS, US government) | SIL OFL 1.1 | https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Public+Sans and https://public-sans.digital.gov/ | Strict neutral grotesque, no quirks, tall x-height, unambiguous 1 l I and 0 O, designed for official notices. Ideal for byelaw posters and safety signage that should look institutional and generic. |
| **Barlow** (Jeremy Tribby) | SIL OFL 1.1 | https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Barlow | Low contrast grotesque explicitly modelled on Californian highway signage. Slightly rounded, huge family (Barlow, Semi Condensed, Condensed). Warm transit flavour. Good for a condensed style directional run. Note the NeoStack plugin also bundles Barlow. |
| **Hind** (Indian Type Foundry) | SIL OFL 1.1 | https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Hind | Humanist sans built for signage and screen legibility, generous x-height, open counters, five weights. A softer, more humane alternative to Overpass if the pure highway look is too hard. |
| **Inter** (Rasmus Andersson) | SIL OFL 1.1 | https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter and https://rsms.me/inter/ | Contemporary UI grotesque, very large x-height, tall figures, excellent at small sizes and on screens. Best for the dynamic displays (next train indicator, help point screens) rather than architectural signage. |

Avoid: P22 Underground (paid Johnston revival, wrong on cost and IP),
googlefonts/railway-sans (an open Johnston clone, the exact thing to avoid),
Lato (often suggested as a Johnston lookalike, too close for comfort here).

Recommendation: **Overpass** for architectural wayfinding and platform numbers,
**Overpass Mono** for the departure board, **Public Sans** for dense notices.
Record the final pick in `docs/art-direction.md`.

## Pictograms

ISO 7010 safety symbols (running person exit, no entry, warning triangle, fire
equipment) are an international standard and are not TfL IP. Public domain and CC
SVGs are on Wikimedia Commons:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/ISO_7010 . Compose way out, line and platform
signage from these plus the chosen typeface on a violet or sodium bar.
