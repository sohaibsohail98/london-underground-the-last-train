# Station architecture

Two real stations sit under Canary Wharf. They are different building types.
Both are recorded here as real-world reference, but the game's arena is
modelled on the **main-line / Elizabeth-line-scale station**, NOT the deep-tube
Jubilee one, because the game's train is a Class 345 "Aventra" (main-line
loading gauge) per `rolling-stock.md` and `docs/brief-v3-unreal.md` Part 1. Use
section 2 (the Elizabeth line box, Grimshaw line-wide platform design) as the
lead for platform width, height and cladding. Section 1 (the Jubilee box) is
kept for its spatial ideas that carry regardless of gauge (the deep descent,
the undivided volume, the wide escalator banks, the under-lit concrete), not as
the platform-tunnel cross-section. All copyrighted images below are linked and
described, not embedded.

## 1. Canary Wharf Jubilee line station (Foster and Partners, opened 1999)

A deep-tube station, kept here for its transferable spatial concepts (the
descent, the single undivided volume, the escalator banks, the moody under-lit
concrete), NOT for its platform-tunnel cross-section. A single vast concrete
box, frequently called cathedral like in scale.

### Overall spatial concept and dimensions

- Box, verified from two sources: **280 m long, 32 m wide, 24 m deep** (Building
  magazine, "The Cathedral", 1999). Wikipedia's Jubilee Line Extension figures
  give the station pit as roughly **265 m long and 24 m deep**; the 24 m depth
  agrees, the length varies with where you measure from. Treat **265 to 300 m
  long, ~30 m wide, ~24 m deep** as the honest range. Foster's own copy rounds it
  to "300 metres long".
- Built by cut and cover in a drained arm of the former West India Dock, inside
  sunk cofferdams. The box is the former dock hollow.
- One undivided volume. Ticket hall and concourse are the same enormous space,
  read as a nave. Comparison repeatedly made: the box laid on end is about the
  height of One Canada Square, the tower above it.
- Designed for around 50,000 passengers a day, exceeded almost immediately
  (69,759 on a weekday recorded in 2006).

### The vertical stack

Three levels, stacked, connected by the escalator banks:

1. **Ticket hall / upper concourse** at the top, lit by daylight from the glass
   canopies. Kiosks, offices, gatelines and amenities are pushed to the flanks
   so the central floor stays clear.
2. **Lower concourse** below it.
3. **Platform level** at the bottom, 24 m down, two platforms with full height
   platform screen doors. The concrete tunnel walls are left exposed and
   fair faced at platform level.

The drama is the descent: you enter at a bright glass canopy in a park, then
drop the full 24 m down long parallel escalator runs into the concrete volume.
This is the single most transferable idea for the arena, see below.

### Escalator banks

- **20 escalators** total, plus 3 lifts, serving the 2 platforms (Wikipedia).
- Arranged as **banks at either end of the concourse**, with an additional group
  described as "five banks at platform level" (Building magazine). Foster's copy
  says "twenty banks of escalators", which is loose wording for 20 units.
- Parallel runs, side by side, four or more wide in a bank, so the descent reads
  as a broad ramp of moving stair rather than a single flight. Pitch is the UK
  standard escalator angle of **30 degrees** (comfort escalators; older Tube
  escalators are 30 degrees, some legacy ones steeper). Model at 30 degrees.
- The width and parallelism is the point. A wide bank of parallel escalators
  descending into a dim concrete hall is instantly "big London interchange".

### Materials

Deliberately simple and hard wearing, chosen for durability and easy
maintenance:

- **Fair faced / board marked in situ concrete**, smooth finish, careful
  aggregate and mix. The architect wanted the pour marks and blemishes visible:
  "a handmade finish that distinguishes it from the other materials". Walls,
  columns, soffit are all this one grey concrete.
- **Exposed structural columns**, a central row, **elliptical / lozenge (oval)
  cross section**, fair faced concrete, carrying the roof. Roughly seven big
  columns are called out down the central spine supporting the curved "gullwing"
  roof slab. Streamlined, not rectangular, not round.
- **Stainless steel** for all the secondary elements: balustrades, handrails,
  cladding trims, kiosk fronts, gateline furniture.
- **Glass** for the entrance canopies and the balustrade infills.
- Large stretches of wall are false frontages hiding service corridors, so
  maintenance happens unseen. Useful: a flat concrete wall plane can be pure
  facade with rooms behind, no need to model depth everywhere.

### Column grid and spacing

- A central single row of the big oval columns down the long axis. Roughly seven
  major columns are described for the roof, which over ~280 m implies a very
  large bay, order of **30 to 40 m centres** for the primary roof columns.
- Secondary structure and platform level will have a tighter grid; exact
  platform column spacing is not published. For the grey box, one heavy oval
  column every ~6 tiles (9 m) down the platform spine reads correctly and keeps
  sightlines. Do not over column it; the real place feels open.

### Ceiling and lighting concept

- A curved concrete roof slab, the "gullwing" section, spanning the box.
- Acoustic treatment: **metal tubes positioned across the soffit to break up
  sound waves**, backed by fibrous acoustic boarding. This gives the ceiling a
  ribbed, slatted texture over the raw concrete. Good detail to model as a
  repeating baffle.
- Artificial lighting was deliberately kept **below the normal code level**. The
  design leans on daylight punched down from the canopies and on "perceived"
  brightness. The result is a moody, under lit concrete hall, which suits a
  survival horror arena directly. Lean into low ambient, pooled light.
- The platform level roundels are 1 m in diameter, the largest on the network
  (this is trade dress, do not reproduce; noted only as a fact about sign scale).

### What translates to the arena

These ideas carry regardless of gauge. For the actual platform cross-section
(width, height, cladding) use section 2, because the game's train is main-line
gauge.

- **Train as a wall:** platform screen doors and the exposed concrete wall
  opposite give a clean hard edge down one long side. The train sits in that
  slot and fills the side. The grid already reserves rows 8 to 24 for this. On a
  main-line-gauge platform this slot is wider and taller than the deep-tube one
  here.
- **Corridor to a vanishing point:** the platform is long, straight, low lit,
  and lined with the repeating oval columns and the ribbed soffit. The horde
  walking out of the dark far end down that colonnade is exactly the reference
  frame.
- **One side closed, one open:** the train and screen doors close the platform
  side; the concourse and escalator bank open off the other side as the fallback
  space.
- **The escalator descent** is the interchange geometry: a wide bank of parallel
  30 degree escalators dropping from a mezzanine into the lower concourse gives
  the second level and a traversal loop, and reads unmistakably as this station.
- **The under lit concrete** is a gift. The reference frame's sodium and
  darkness is close to the real lighting brief.

## 2. Canary Wharf Elizabeth line station, under Crossrail Place (Foster and Partners with Arup, structure; Grimshaw line-wide platform design; opened for the Elizabeth line 2022, retail 2015)

**This is the lead reference for the game's platform**, because the game's train
is a main-line-gauge Class 345. A deep concrete box in the dock with a long
timber roofed retail and park structure floating over it.

### Overall concept and dimensions

- The station box: a **475 m long concrete box** in West India North Dock on an
  artificial island, with a **245 m island platform** fitted out to about 210 m.
  Sits roughly **30 m below dock water level**. Foundations: 293 interlocking
  steel piles 18.5 m high and 1.2 m wide, plus 38 m deep concrete piles.
  (Wikipedia, Canary Wharf railway station.)
- Above the platforms: **five levels** of mixed use, "Crossrail Place", four
  retail levels and a roof garden park on top, all in the middle of the dock.
- Over the whole thing: the **timber lattice roof, 310 m long, arching about
  30 m over the park**, wrapping down around the shops and entrances.

### The timber lattice roof, and how it differs

- Structure: **1,418 spruce glulam beams, 564 steel nodes** (364 unique). Beam
  lengths roughly 4 to 7.5 m (13 to 25 ft). Diagonal beams about 33 in (840 mm)
  deep, horizontal beams about 18 in (460 mm) deep.
- Only **four curved timber beams in the entire roof**; the smooth curve is
  faked from straight beams meeting at the bespoke steel nodes.
- Clad in **ETFE air cushions**, not glass: lighter, translucent, pillowy.
- It is an open lattice, half enclosure, with big triangular and diamond
  openings letting rain and light onto the planting. Warm timber colour, a
  strong contrast to the cool grey concrete of the Jubilee box.
- This is a canopy over a retail street, not an enclosed station hall. For the
  game it is the reference for an **above ground entrance / mezzanine** with a
  distinctive lattice ceiling, not for the platform arena itself.

### Elizabeth line platform environment (Grimshaw line-wide design)

This is the platform the game's arena is modelled on (main-line gauge, matching
the Aventra):

- Platforms and passenger tunnels are near **double the length and height** of a
  standard Tube station.
- Clad in **creamy glass fibre reinforced concrete (GFRC) panels** that shrink
  wrap the sprayed concrete tunnel lining, held on a **stainless steel ladder
  frame**. Curved junctions everywhere, no sharp internal corners, long
  sightlines.
- Aesthetic stated as raw materials, concrete reflecting the concrete structure
  behind, not painted surfaces. Glass and stainless steel for everything
  touchable. Full height platform edge screen doors.

### What translates to the arena

- The **GFRC curved tunnel cladding** with its continuous coved corners is the
  default platform wall treatment: smoother, paler, more clinical than raw
  board-marked concrete. Raw concrete can still feature on the opposite (track)
  wall or in back-of-house.
- The **oversized double-height main-line tunnel** is the model for the platform
  hall itself and for a wide, tall spawn corridor feeding it. This is the "tall
  tiled corridor receding to a vanishing point" of the reference frame.
- The **timber lattice** is a memorable ceiling for the upper concourse or
  entrance box if you want to distinguish the mezzanine from the platform.

## Quick number table for blockout

| Element | Jubilee box | Elizabeth box |
|---|---|---|
| Overall length | 265 to 300 m | 475 m box, 245 m platform |
| Width | ~32 m | double a standard Tube tunnel |
| Depth below surface / water | 24 m | ~30 m below dock water |
| Escalators | 20 units, banks each end + platform level | multiple long banks, standard Elizabeth line |
| Escalator pitch | 30 degrees | 30 degrees |
| Primary columns | central row, oval section, ~7 major, tens of m apart | GFRC clad, curved cladding, few visible columns |
| Ceiling | curved concrete gullwing slab, acoustic tube baffles | 310 m timber glulam lattice + ETFE over the retail deck; GFRC coved soffit at platform |
| Lighting | deliberately below code, moody | even, integrated cove lighting |
| Levels | ticket hall / lower concourse / platform | platform + 5 levels of retail and park above |
