# Station signage and furniture, dimensional reference

Extracted 2026-09-09 from three published Transport for London design
standards. This is the measurements half of station wayfinding: panel sizes,
cap heights, border widths, corner radii, mounting heights, materials and
finishes. It exists so the Phase F signage work is built at real world scale
instead of eyeballed.

## Read this before using any of it

The measurements are functional specifications. A 250 by 200 mm enamel panel
with a 4 mm corner radius and a 2 mm white border is a manufacturing fact, and
building a sign to that size is what makes a station read as a station.

The identity laid over the top of those measurements is not ours to take, and
none of it appears below. Specifically:

- **No roundel.** The circle and bar mark is registered and licensed. The
  project's substitute is an original station mark that is deliberately not a
  circle and bar silhouette. See `art-direction.md`.
- **No Johnston, New Johnston or Johnston 100.** The corporate typeface is
  exclusive to the operator and licensed on application. The substitute is a
  geometric sans that is not a Johnston clone. Hammersmith One under the SIL
  Open Font Licence is the closest freely licensed candidate and is not a
  clone, but check it against the mark before committing to it.
- **No reproduction of the official line diagram.**
- **No operator wordmark, logotype or livery.**

`branding-precedent.md` sets out why real geography plus an invented marque is
the normal route rather than an unusually cautious one.

The sources are public documents and are not committed to this repository.

## 1. The grammar of an enamel sign panel

Every safety and information panel in the supplementary standard is built from
the same few moves. Get these right and an invented sign reads as authentic
without borrowing anything.

| Element | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Corner radius, small panels | 4 mm external | Panels up to about 300 mm |
| Corner radius, large panels | 8 mm external | Panels 450 mm and wider |
| Corner radius, platform barrier panels | 72.5 mm external | The long end-of-platform panels |
| Corner radius, escalator panels | 10 mm external | |
| White border, small panels | 2 mm | Runs the full perimeter |
| White border, large panels | 4 mm | |
| White border between stacked blocks | 6 mm | Multi-block warning panels |
| White border, flanged panels | 8 mm | The extra allows for the flange |
| Prohibition ring and bar | 150 mm diameter on 750 mm panels, 50 to 85 mm on small ones | |
| Pictogram block | 118 by 118 mm or 150 by 150 mm | Two standard squares |

Panels are stacked as coloured blocks separated by white rule, not as one field
with mixed content. A three-hazard panel is three full width colour bands.

## 3. Mounting heights

| Item | Height | Measured to |
|---|---|---|
| Priority seat sign | 1500 mm | Bottom edge, centred over the seat |
| Forecourt cycle and parking signs | 1600 mm | Bottom edge |
| Platform prohibition signs | 2000 mm | Top edge, where possible |

Two placement rules worth copying because they change how a platform looks:

- Prohibition signs go **over alternate** trackside name panels, never over
  every one. A platform carries at most eight.
- Surveillance notices go at platform entry and exit points, **not at every
  camera**. The standard warns explicitly that too many signs make an area feel
  hostile and raise anxiety. For this project that is a lever, not a warning:
  oversigning is a cheap way to make a station feel wrong.

## 4. Materials and finishes

Straight from the products standard. These are the material assignments for
Phase F1 and F5 props.

| Item | Construction |
|---|---|
| Safety and information panels | Flat vitreous enamel; flanged vitreous enamel where fixed to barriers |
| Modular sign frames | Cast aluminium corners with extruded cut lengths, internal hinges |
| Directional signage | Aluminium frame with pressed aluminium panels |
| Way out signs | The only internally illuminated panels in the system |
| Exterior station marks | Cast and sheet aluminium with polycarbonate, internally illuminated |
| Totems | Aluminium and stainless steel, stove enamel finish, internally illuminated |
| Poster frames | Aluminium extrusion with 4 mm safety glass front |
| Digital clocks | Cast aluminium, non-reflective safety glass, IP65, transflective LCD |
| Matrix displays | Sheet aluminium, safety glass, LED |
| Free-standing units | Sheet and angle steel with cast aluminium base, powder coated |
| Finger posts | Extruded aluminium post, stainless steel or powder coated mild steel sleeve |
| Temporary signage | Screen printed self-adhesive UV stable vinyl |

Three finish notes that matter more than they sound:

- Vitreous enamel is glossy, hard and self-cleaning. It stays clean while
  everything around it does not. On a grimy platform the signs are the
  cleanest surfaces in frame, which is the opposite of the usual instinct to
  weather everything evenly.
- Powder coat and stove enamel are matt to satin, not glossy.
- Products are specified with **no horizontal surfaces within reach**, to
  discourage litter and prevent concealment of packages. Every bench, frame
  and totem is either sloped or flush. Modelling a flat ledge anywhere below
  head height reads as wrong even when nobody can say why.

## 5. Legibility, and the rule the whole system rests on

The products standard gives one number that drives everything else:

> **One metre of viewing distance per 2 mm of digit height.**

A 100 mm digit is legible at 50 m. Invert it for the game: work out the sight
line from the platform entrance to the departure board in Unreal units, divide
by 500, and that is the minimum cap height in metres for the board to be
readable at the far end. Anything smaller is decoration.

The matrix display font is described in the source as a close replica of the
corporate typeface rather than the typeface itself, which is a useful precedent:
even the operator uses a substitute where the real face will not render.

## 6. Typographic policy

Developed with the Royal National Institute of Blind People. These are
accessibility principles, freely reusable, and following them is what makes
invented signage look institutional rather than fan made.

- Mixed upper and lower case throughout, **never all capitals**, not even in
  headings.
- Ranged left, ragged right. Never justified, never ranged right.
- Centred only on posters, and only where the body copy is at most three lines.
- Horizontal only. Never vertical, never angled, never on a curve.
- No italics. Emphasise with a different weight.
- Do not adjust tracking. Do not reduce leading.
- No first line indent. Separate paragraphs with extra leading.
- Minimum point size is set by viewing distance, not by taste.
- Strong contrast between text and background, always.

Note the tension with the game's own material: the destination blind and the
platform name panel in the reference frame are set in capitals. Real practice is
mixed case. Follow the reference frame for the hero shot and this policy for
everything the player reads up close.

### 7.3 The safety colour roles

The safety colours are British and European standard hazard colours, not
operator property, and the **roles** are what to copy. Sign colour tells you
what kind of instruction it is before you can read it.

| Role | Colour | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibition | Safety red, ring and bar on white | No smoking, no entry, no busking |
| Warning | Safety yellow, black symbol and border | Fast trains, electrical hazard, end of platform |
| Mandatory and informational | Safety blue, white symbol | Priority seat, help point, surveillance |
| Safe condition and escape | Safety green, white symbol | First aid, stretcher, assembly point |
| Fire equipment | Safety red field, white text | Fire point, fire call point |

Colour first, symbol second, text last. A player scanning a platform under
emergency lighting should read the hazard from the colour block alone.

## 8. What to build from this

For `docs/tasks/phase-f5-signage-wayfinding.md`:

1. One master material for vitreous enamel: glossy, hard, self-cleaning, barely
   weathered even where the walls are filthy.
2. A panel mesh set at the sizes in section 2, sharing the corner radii and
   border widths in section 1. Six or seven panel sizes cover the whole
   station.
3. An original station mark, not a circle and bar, applied at the mounting
   positions in section 3 including the alternate-panel rule.
4. A geometric sans that is not a Johnston clone, set mixed case, ranged left,
   at cap heights driven by the legibility rule in section 5.
5. Safety panels using the colour roles in section 7.3 and invented wording.
   The wording is ours; the colour grammar is standard.
