# Branding precedent — how other games handled TfL IP

Supporting research for `docs/art-direction.md`. That document says what to
substitute. This one says why the substitution is the normal path rather than an
unusually cautious one.

## The two routes

TfL runs a formal brand licensing programme. Usage is briefed to
`copyright@tfl.gov.uk` and licensing of the map is administered through a
partner with set royalty rates. Infringements go to `IPRInfringements@tfl.gov.uk`.
So the roundel, the Johnston and New Johnston typefaces, and the official line
diagram are all licensable, and all licensed.

Two shipped games show both routes.

### Watch Dogs: Legion — licensed

Legion uses the real roundel in-game, which commentators noted at the time as
surprising given TfL's historic protectiveness over the mark. Ubisoft licensed
it. The Underground appears as the fast travel network, and stations are a mix
of real and invented ones, with fictional stations such as Tidis sitting
alongside real and under-construction ones like Nine Elms.

Not a route available to this project at any sensible cost.

### Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 3 — unlicensed

MW3 included a sequence set on the Tube and was unable to use any TfL branding
at all. A major publisher shipped a recognisable Underground level with zero
TfL marks on it.

That is the direct precedent for Last Train, and it is the route this project
takes.

## What the precedent actually licenses us to do

The useful lesson is the split Legion makes: **real geography, invented marque.**
Station names, line names, adjacency and the physical shape of a station are
factual and unprotected. What is protected is the identity system laid over the
top: the roundel, the typefaces, the diagram, the recorded announcements.

So the design rule stays as written in `art-direction.md`:

- Original station mark, deliberately not a circle-and-bar silhouette.
- A geometric sans that is not a Johnston clone.
- Original announcement phrasing, spoken by no named announcer.
- Fictional advertising throughout.
- Station names and geography used freely.

## Asset reuse from those games

Not available, and not worth revisiting.

- Watch Dogs: Legion assets are Ubisoft's, unlicensed for reuse in any form.
- Fallout: London is a Fallout 4 total conversion by Team FOLON. Its Nexus
  permissions require explicit permission before using any asset from the file
  and forbid conversion to another game outright. The assets are also Creation
  Engine formats and would not port.

Everything in this project is either built by us, generated in engine, or drawn
from a CC0 source recorded in `assets/README.md`.

## If the project ever goes commercial

Revisit this page before any paid release. Two things change: the fictional
advertising needs a pass for accidental resemblance to real campaigns, and it
becomes worth a single briefing email to TfL to confirm the substitutions read
as clearly distinct rather than as a roundel with the serial numbers filed off.
